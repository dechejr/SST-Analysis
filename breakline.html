What's a break?

A temperature break is where two bodies of water meet — warm Gulf Stream water against cooler shelf water, or the edge of an eddy that's spun off the main current. Bait gets pushed along these edges, and predators follow. The tighter the color change on the chart, the sharper the break.

Reading the gauge

Color shows sea surface temperature, from cold (deep blue/purple) to warm (red). This isn't a nautical chart — think of it as an overlay for spotting structure in the water itself. Values are approximate; confirm with your boat's temp gauge on site.

On the water

  • Work the up-current edge of a warm eddy first — bait tends to stack there.
  • A break that's held steady for a few days usually fishes better than one that just formed.
  • Color alone won't show current speed or clarity — pair this with local reports.

About this data

Composite is the MUR analysis (JPL/PO.DAAC) — satellite passes blended into one gap-free global picture at ~1km resolution, updated once a day with about a day of lag.

Hourly is read directly off GOES-19, NOAA's geostationary weather satellite parked over the Americas. It updates every hour but only sees through clear sky, so cloudy stretches will show gaps until the next clear pass.

Tap anywhere on the water to drop a mark — if a reading is available at that point, the temperature shows up right in the mark card.

About this prototype

This is a working preview built for the web so you can try it right now — no app-store install needed. A native iOS/Android build would add push alerts when a break forms near a saved spot, offline chart caching, and on-device GPS. Your saved spots persist privately across visits.
